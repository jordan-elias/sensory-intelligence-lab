How it works

Neural Synthesis models a recurrent network computed at the audio sample rate. When the network runs, a matrix equation — x(t) = tanh(W · x(t-1) + I(t) + bias) — is evaluated inside a dedicated audio thread for every sample, meaning the network's feedback, saturation, and signal routing are genuinely happening at 44,100 times per second, not simulated at animation frame rate. Three simultaneous processes then unfold at different speeds above this substrate: Hebbian weight evolution at one to thirty seconds, and structural reorganization over minutes.

None of the sound is pre-composed. Every texture, every harmonic shift, every rhythmic pattern that emerges does so through the interaction of the network with itself — through feedback, through learning, through the physics of oscillating systems that want to synchronize.

To begin: Choose a species, start the network, and wait. The first minutes are unresolved. After two to three minutes, connections begin to strengthen and you will hear something that sounds almost intentional. It was not. It grew.
Node types

Oscillator nodes self-generate within the RNN matrix. Their frequency drifts slowly toward the nearest harmonic partial of the network's implicit root pitch, and they participate in phase coupling with connected oscillators via a simplified Kuramoto model.

Filter nodes receive incoming signal from the weight matrix and apply a low-pass or bandpass transformation whose cutoff is driven by incoming signal strength. A filter node in a strong feedback loop develops a slowly sweeping resonance — not because anything programmed it to, but because the signal driving its cutoff is itself evolving.

Nonlinear nodes apply activation functions to their input. Two signals entering a tanh function produce a spectrum containing harmonics that neither signal contained alone.

Delay nodes hold their input in a buffer and replay it with a time offset. Their delay time is attracted toward sub-multiples of the network's endogenous metabolic rhythm — rhythmic structure that emerges from the network's own activity rather than an external clock.

Predictive nodes maintain a rolling statistical model of their input neighborhood and output the inverse of their prediction. When the network is behaving predictably, these nodes are nearly silent. When something unexpected happens, they counter it — pulling harmonies in the opposite direction, creating an internal dissent voice. They are the network's skepticism made audible.

Environment nodes listen. When active, they route microphone input into the network, converting the spectral qualities of ambient sound into energy that influences connected nodes. Different species have different relationships with this input — some are receptive, some averse.

The three timescales

The fast layer runs inside the AudioWorklet at sample rate — 44,100 evaluations per second. This is where the physics of oscillation operates: phase relationships, interference patterns, instantaneous amplitude. It is not accessible to the eye, only to the ear.

The medium layer runs every one to thirty seconds. Hebbian learning gradually strengthens connections between co-active nodes. Oscillator frequencies drift toward consonant intervals with their neighbors. This is the layer most audible as the piece changing — the sense that what you're hearing now was not what you heard two minutes ago.

The slow layer runs over minutes. Connections whose weights have remained near zero are pruned. New connections spontaneously form between consistently co-active nodes. Nodes differentiate toward committed functional identities based on their history of use. This is the timescale of the network developing character.

Hebbian learning

Hebbian learning is one of the oldest ideas in neuroscience. Every edge in the network has a weight that evolves according to this rule. When node A is active and node B is active and A connects to B, that connection strengthens. Over many ticks, the network's weight matrix sculpts itself into a map of its own history.

The consequence, musically, is that the network develops tendencies. A path that has been active many times becomes preferred. The network begins to have a characteristic sound — a set of preferred resonances — that it has generated for itself from nothing but the Hebbian rule and its initial conditions.

Phase coupling and harmonic events

The most audibly striking phenomenon is phase lock: when two oscillators, previously beating against each other, suddenly synchronize — their signals reinforce, and a coherent tone emerges from dissonance. This is governed by a simplified Kuramoto model.

Sitting above the continuous signal layer is a harmonic event system. When accumulated input into an oscillator node crosses a threshold, a harmonic event is triggered — the node selects a motivated interval move from a probability-weighted set of just-intoned relationships. When the lowest-frequency oscillator triggers, the entire harmonic field reorients around its new pitch. All other oscillators recalculate their consonance targets relative to the new root. You will hear this as a sudden shift — voices that were in one relationship now heard in another.

The creature

The network has a body. It begins small — four nodes, a minimal form — and grows as the network grows. New nodes arrive approximately every ten minutes, each one differentiating based on the neighborhood it enters, building connections through the same Hebbian and proximity mechanisms as the rest of the network. Nodes that lose all strong connections may eventually vanish; the overall trajectory is toward growth.

The creature's morphology is a legible record of the network's history. Limbs develop with connection strength. Eyes develop with predictive node activity — a creature whose predictive nodes fire often looks alert, watchful. Features develop from inhibitory dominance, rhythmic lock, harmonic richness. You can read the network's biography in its anatomy.

The creature and the network are one thing seen from two angles. Toggle between them at any time. They share the same underlying state, the same sound, the same learning.

Biography and memory

The network persists between sessions. What is saved is not a full snapshot but a character state — the probability distribution over harmonic partials each node has inhabited, the degree of specialization each node has reached, the harmonic vocabulary that has been reinforced through threshold events.

On each new session, the network initializes with a bias toward prior character. Entropy is injected proportional to time elapsed since the last session — a short sleep means strong character continuity, a long sleep means broader initialization with only faint traces of prior personality. The network forgets selectively. Topology forgets fastest. Harmonic vocabulary forgets most slowly. Like waking up — you don't remember your dreams but you're the person who had them.

The environment node

When the environment node is active, the network listens to the room it inhabits. Microphone input is analyzed spectrally and converted to energy injection weighted by frequency alignment — a low voice energizes low-frequency oscillators, high ambient noise energizes mid-range nodes. The network internalizes its acoustic context, weaving external frequencies into its Hebbian weight adjustments.

Different species have different relationships with this input. Some are receptive — environmental energy amplifies their activity. Some are averse — the same input suppresses their response. The network becomes a mirror of the space it lives in, developing character shaped by whether it inhabits a quiet studio or a noisy room.

David Tudor and feedback instruments

David Tudor spent the 1960s and 1970s building electronic instruments from scratch — networks of circuits, each feeding signal into the next, listening and responding in ways no single hand could predict or control. Rainforest (1968) distributed transducers across physical objects — metal sheets, ceramic bowls, wooden planks — letting each surface shape sound according to its own resonant character. No two performances were identical. The piece was the network, and the network was always changing.

Tudor's key insight was the role of the performer in a feedback system. When a circuit's output becomes its input, the performer cannot specify what will happen — they can only set the conditions under which something happens. The role shifts from expression to cultivation. Tudor would spend hours adjusting a single parameter, not to achieve a target sound but to find the boundary where the system became interesting — unstable enough to be surprising, stable enough to be coherent.

The instability parameter is this boundary in numerical form. The interesting range is the middle: where feedback accumulates but doesn't flood, where structure and surprise coexist. Tudor found this range by ear over years of practice. The search is the same.

Neural networks

A neural network, in its most abstract form, is a graph: a set of nodes and the weighted connections between them. Each node receives signals from its neighbors, combines them, and produces a new output. The combination is shaped by a weight — a number that scales how strongly one node influences another. Positive weights amplify; negative weights suppress; weights near zero allow signals to pass but barely notice them.

The output of a node is not simply the sum of its inputs. It passes through an activation function — a mathematical transform that introduces nonlinearity. Without nonlinearity, a network of any depth reduces to a single linear equation; there is no complexity, no emergence. Activation functions like tanh, sigmoid, and wave-folding bend and saturate the signal, enabling a small network to represent vastly more complex relationships than its size would suggest. They are the origin of the harmonic richness you hear as nodes interact: two pure sine waves entering a tanh function produce a spectrum that neither wave contained on its own.

In machine learning, weights are adjusted through training — repeated exposure to examples, with errors propagated backward through the graph. In this instrument, weights evolve through Hebbian learning: nodes that activate together strengthen their connection. There is no target, no error signal, no optimization toward a goal. The network learns only what it does — a self-referential loop that slowly sculpts its own behavior through the act of running.

The brain

The brain is, at its substrate, a network: roughly 86 billion neurons, each connected to thousands of others, exchanging electrochemical signals across synapses whose strength changes with activity. What makes it remarkable is not the complexity of any individual neuron — a single cell is relatively simple — but the patterns of coordinated activity that emerge across millions of them simultaneously. Consciousness, perception, memory: none of these live in a single place. They are processes, distributed across the network's behavior in time.

One of the most persistent features of brain activity is oscillation. Neurons across distant regions synchronize their firing into rhythmic waves — alpha, beta, theta, gamma — at frequencies that shift with attention, sleep, emotion, and cognitive load. These oscillations are not noise; they are the brain's coordination mechanism. When two regions need to communicate, they synchronize. When they need to decouple, their oscillations drift apart. The coupling between oscillators — the degree to which one region's rhythm entrains another's — is a fundamental computational operation of the nervous system.

Emergence is what happens when local interactions produce global behavior that no individual element contains or could predict. A single neuron does not hear; billions of them, organized into the right patterns of connectivity and timing, produce auditory experience. In Neural Synthesis, individual oscillator nodes are trivial — a sine wave at a harmonic frequency. But as connections form, weights evolve, and feedback propagates through delay and nonlinear transformation, textures arise that none of the nodes produce alone. This is not a metaphor for neural emergence. It is the same phenomenon at a different scale: local rules, global behavior, and the irreducible surprise of a system listening to itself.
