How these exercises work


Each breathing pattern is drawn from clinical and research contexts where voluntary breath control has been shown to influence autonomic nervous system activity — specifically the balance between sympathetic (activating) and parasympathetic (calming) responses.


The extended exhale used in the 4–7–8 breath and the physiological sigh increases vagal tone, which is associated with reduced heart rate and a shift away from stress arousal. Box breathing's symmetrical structure is used in high-performance and clinical settings for sustained regulation. Resonant breathing targets the specific frequency (~0.1 Hz) at which heart rate variability peaks, producing measurable coherence between cardiac and respiratory rhythm.


The accompanying soundscape follows the shape of each breath: the fundamental tone and harmonic richness expand on inhale and contract on exhale, giving the sound the same quality of opening and closing as the breath itself. You do not need to consciously follow the pattern — the visual and sound are designed to entrain the body gently.
