About Éliane Radigue

Éliane Radigue (1932–2016) was a pioneer of electronic music who worked with the ARP 2500 synthesizer in the 1970s and 1980s. Rather than working with conventional synthesis techniques, she used the instrument to create slowly evolving, hypnotic drones — pieces that could last 20, 30, or even 40 minutes with imperceptible changes accumulating across the duration. She believed in patience, in listening deeply to tiny variations, in music as a meditation.

How this tool works

Three sine wave oscillators are tuned slightly apart from each other. Because they're not perfectly in tune, they create "beating" patterns at their difference frequencies — you'll hear this as a slow wavering or shimmering effect. Each oscillator also has its own LFO (low-frequency oscillator) modulating its frequency slightly, causing the beating pattern to evolve over time.

The drift rate controls how quickly the LFOs change the frequencies. Lower drift = very slow, almost imperceptible change. Higher drift = faster, more audible morphing.

Listening techniques

Low volume: Play the drone very quietly. Many of Radigue's pieces are only fully appreciated at soft levels — tiny variations become audible when there's less absolute loudness.

Deep listening: Let the piece play for 5–10 minutes before deciding what you're hearing. The changes are gradual; it takes time for patterns to emerge.

Find interference patterns: Listen for the rhythmic pulsing created by the three oscillators interfering with each other. As drift changes the frequencies, these patterns shift and evolve.

Parameter guide

Base Freq: The fundamental pitch. Try lower frequencies (60–100 Hz) for meditative drones; higher frequencies (150–200 Hz) for brighter, more tense pieces.

Detuning: How far apart the oscillators are. Narrow detuning (0.5–1.5 Hz) = very subtle beating. Wide detuning (4–8 Hz) = more obvious, faster pulsing.

Drift Rate: How quickly the LFOs modulate. Ultra-low (0.01–0.03) feels almost static. Medium (0.05–0.1) feels alive. High (0.2+) feels quite active.

Noise Floor: A small amount of noise can add texture and prevent the piece from sounding too "synthetic." Try very small amounts (off to low).

Creating your own composition

Experiment with combinations: a low base frequency with very narrow detuning and extremely slow drift can create almost meditative pieces. A higher base with wider detuning and faster drift can be more active and surprising. Save your favorite settings as presets for later exploration.

Download & use

Generate a 5, 10, or 20-minute version. These pieces work well as ambient background, as material for further sound design, or as a basis for improvisation or layering with other sounds.
