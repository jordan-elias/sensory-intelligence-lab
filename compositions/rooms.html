How it works

In "I Am Sitting in a Room," Alvin Lucier repeatedly played his voice through a speaker and microphone, recording each iteration. Over time, the room's natural resonances—determined by its dimensions and materials—became more and more prominent, while his voice gradually disappeared.

Here, you choose a room (small room, bathroom, kitchen, or concert hall), each with different resonant frequencies. Upload a file, record yourself, or use a generated sample. Press + iterate to process the current sound through the room's resonances. After several iterations, you'll hear the room's acoustic signature emerge.

Choosing a room

Small room: Lower resonances, tighter spaces. Faster emergence of acoustic character.

Bathroom: Bright, reflective surfaces. Higher frequencies are amplified; creates a characteristic "bathroom" tone.

Kitchen: Mixed hard and soft surfaces. Subtle, balanced resonances.

Concert hall: Large space with gentle resonances. Slower, more gradual transformation.

About the loop feature

The loop button makes the current iteration repeat continuously. This lets you sit with a single iteration and listen for emerging patterns before deciding whether to process it further.

Downloading your work

Use the download section to export any range of iterations as a single WAV file, optionally looped. Select a starting iteration, ending iteration, and number of loops. This lets you create a composition showing the complete progression from source to resonance.

Try it in a real room

The physical version is simple: record yourself speaking on one device. Play that recording back through a speaker in a real room and record it on a second device with the microphone placed in the room. Play that recording back and record again. Repeat 8-10 times to hear the room's resonances emerge.
