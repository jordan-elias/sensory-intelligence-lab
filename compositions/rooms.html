How it works

In "I Am Sitting in a Room" (1969), Alvin Lucier read a text aloud, played it back through a speaker in a room, re-recorded it with a microphone, and repeated this process. With each pass, the room's natural resonant frequencies — determined by its dimensions — were amplified, while the original sound was gradually smoothed away. After enough iterations, only pure tones remained.

Upload a file or record yourself speaking, then press + iterate to pass the sound through the acoustic simulation. Each iteration reinforces the room's resonant frequencies while suppressing everything else. By the third iteration you will notice tonal coloring; by the tenth, only resonances remain.

Using the waveform editor

When you upload or record audio, a waveform editor appears. Drag the handles to select a portion of the audio, preview your selection, then click Use Selection to set it as your loop. Trim silence at the beginning or end, or isolate a spoken phrase. A shorter loop produces faster, more dramatic results.

Iterating

Each press of + iterate processes the current iteration through the room simulation. The iteration counter and progress bar show where you are. You can iterate up to 25 times. After processing, the new iteration plays automatically. Use the loop button (on by default) to hear any iteration repeat.

Downloading your work

Select a range of iterations and a loop count, then click download wav. The exported file stitches those iterations together in sequence — a complete recording of the transformation from speech to pure resonance.

Listening guide

Iteration 0 is your source. By iteration 3–4, you will hear a resonant coloring begin to emerge. By iteration 8–10, speech or melody will be largely subsumed by the room's characteristic frequencies. By iteration 15–20, only smooth, organ-like tones remain. The process mirrors the original: the room, not the performer, becomes the composer.
