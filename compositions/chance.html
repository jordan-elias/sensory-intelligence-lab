About John Cage and chance

John Cage believed that music didn't need to express the composer's personal emotions or intentions. Instead, he used chance procedures — throwing dice, consulting the I Ching, or drawing random numbers — to make compositional decisions. This shifted control away from the composer and toward the unpredictability of the process itself.

How to use this tool

Select which musical parameters (pitch, rhythm, timbre, volume, panning) you want controlled by chance. Then set the ranges and options for each — the scale, note durations, total length, tempo, and more. When you click "Generate," the tool uses random numbers to create a unique composition within those constraints. The result is never quite what you expect, but always created according to rules you defined.

Interpretations

You can generate as many variations as you like. Each one is valid. Save them, listen to them in different contexts, share them. The point isn't perfection—it's to experience music as a process of constraints and emergence rather than intentional design.

About the parameters

Density: How likely a note is to start at each rhythmic position. Higher density = more notes.
Timing method: Sequential places notes one after another; random onset lets them appear unpredictably.
Timbre: When enabled, waveforms are chosen randomly per note.
Amplitude: Each note gets a random volume within a reasonable range.
Panning: Notes are randomly placed in the stereo field.
