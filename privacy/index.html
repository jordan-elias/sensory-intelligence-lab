Privacy Policy

Your data belongs to you.

Privacy is not a feature added at the end — it is the starting point of how this platform was built. This document explains in plain language what data is collected, how it is stored, how it is protected, and what you can do with it. Technical detail is included deliberately, because vague reassurances are not reassuring.

If something here is unclear, or if you have a question not answered below, write to hello@jordanelias.de. You will get a direct reply.

Who made this

The Sensory Intelligence Lab was built by Jordan Elias, a music therapist based in Berlin. During the pandemic, continuing to work with clients remotely raised a question that had no satisfying answer: where do people go when access to consistent, embodied therapeutic support is disrupted? Video calls were improvised, sessions moved online, and something important was lost in the translation.

That question became this platform — an attempt to build something that could hold continuity between sessions, support self-directed practice, and make music therapy tools accessible to people who cannot easily access in-person care. The timing feels increasingly urgent. Economic precarity is reshaping who can access mental health support. The gig economy is coming for therapists. AI is being positioned as a substitute for human care in ways that are not always honest about what is lost. This platform is not a replacement for therapy. It is a companion to it — and a small argument that access and quality are not mutually exclusive.

Jordan Elias is the data controller for this platform. All questions, requests, and concerns go directly to one person, not a support team.

How encryption works

Sensitive content you create on this platform — journal entries and check-in notes — is encrypted in your browser before it is sent to any server. What is stored in the database is ciphertext: scrambled data that cannot be read without the correct key. This section explains the method in detail.

The encryption standard

All content encryption uses AES-256-GCM (Advanced Encryption Standard, 256-bit key, Galois/Counter Mode). This is the same standard used by banks, governments, and end-to-end encrypted messaging applications. GCM mode provides both confidentiality (the content cannot be read) and integrity (any tampering with the ciphertext is detectable).

Technical specification

Algorithm: AES-256-GCM

Key length: 256 bits (32 bytes)

IV (Initialisation Vector): 96 bits (12 bytes), randomly generated per encryption operation

Implementation: Web Crypto API (window.crypto.subtle) — browser-native, no external libraries

Storage format: JSON object containing base64-encoded IV and ciphertext, stored as a text column in Supabase

Your encryption key

Each account has a unique AES-256 encryption key, generated randomly when you first visit the journal or check-in page. This key is generated on our server (a Netlify serverless function) and returned to your browser over HTTPS, where it is held in memory for the duration of your session. It is never written to localStorage, cookies, or any other persistent browser storage. When you sign out or close the tab, the key is gone from your device.

The key itself is stored in the database in an encrypted form. It is wrapped using a key derived from a platform secret — a random value stored only in our server environment variables, never in the database. When you sign in, our server verifies your identity using your Supabase session token, then uses the platform secret to unwrap your encryption key and return it to your browser.

What this means honestly

Honest limitation This model protects your data against database breaches — if someone obtained a copy of the database, they would see only ciphertext that they cannot decrypt without also compromising the server environment. However, as the platform operator, Jordan Elias has access to the platform secret. This means that, in theory, an operator with access to both the database and the server environment could decrypt your content. This is disclosed because honest privacy policy matters more than false reassurance.
What this protects against Database breaches or leaks where only database content is exposed will not expose your journal entries or check-in notes — they are ciphertext without the server secret. Your data is never stored or logged in plaintext anywhere in the system.

The future upgrade path

The architecture is designed to support an upgrade to true end-to-end encryption in a future version, where your key would be derived from a passphrase only you know, making decryption impossible even for the platform operator. This upgrade path is built into the codebase but not yet activated. It will be announced clearly when available, and upgrading will be opt-in.

Sharing with your therapist

If you choose to share a journal entry, check-in summary, worksheet, or audio file with your therapist, that item is re-encrypted using the practice's RSA-OAEP public key before being sent. This is a form of hybrid encryption: a one-time AES session key encrypts the content, and that session key is encrypted with the RSA public key. Only the practice's private key — held by the therapist and never stored on any server — can decrypt the session key and therefore the content. Sharing is always an explicit, deliberate action. Nothing is shared automatically.

Identity separation

Your identity — email address and name — is stored separately from your content. Supabase maintains a dedicated auth.users table for authentication data (email, hashed password, session tokens). This table is managed directly by Supabase's authentication service and is separate from the profiles table and all content tables (journal_entries, checkins, etc.).

All content tables reference you only by a UUID (universally unique identifier) — a random string like a3f2c1d4-... — not by your name or email. This means that even within the database, your journal entries are not directly labelled with your name. They are linked to an opaque identifier that maps to your identity only via the authentication layer. Someone who obtained only the content tables would see encrypted ciphertext attached to anonymous UUIDs.

Supabase and the database

This platform uses Supabase as its database and authentication provider. Supabase is an open-source Firebase alternative built on PostgreSQL, hosted on AWS infrastructure. The database is hosted in the EU West (Frankfurt) region, keeping data within the European Union.

Row Level Security (RLS)

Every table in the database has Row Level Security enabled. RLS is a PostgreSQL feature that enforces access control at the database level — not just in application code. This means that even if there were a bug in the application layer, the database itself would refuse to return data that the requesting user is not authorised to see.

RLS policy structure

journal_entries: Users can only read, create, and delete rows where user_id = auth.uid(). No other user, including the platform operator querying via the application, can read another user's rows via the standard API.

checkins: Same pattern — rows are visible only to the user who created them.

profiles: Users can read and update their own profile row. Practice members (therapists) can read a restricted view of client profiles that excludes encryption key columns.

shared_items: Users control their own shared items. Practice members with therapist role can read items shared with their practice. The mark-as-viewed operation runs via a database function that restricts what can be modified.

The service role key

Supabase provides two API keys: an anon key (safe to use in the browser, subject to RLS) and a service role key (bypasses RLS, for server-side use only). The service role key is stored only in Netlify environment variables and is never exposed to the browser or included in client-side code. The vault function that handles encryption keys uses the service role key to read and write the wrapped key — this is the only server-side operation that bypasses RLS, and it does so only after verifying the user's JWT.

Supabase's own privacy commitments

Supabase is SOC 2 Type 2 compliant and GDPR-ready. Their infrastructure runs on AWS with encryption at rest and in transit. You can read Supabase's privacy policy at supabase.com/privacy. As the data controller, Jordan Elias is the party responsible for how your data is used — Supabase acts as a data processor.

It is protected by RLS policies that restrict access to you and your assigned therapist, and is transmitted over HTTPS.

Your rights under GDPR

As a resident of the European Union (or the UK, Switzerland, or another jurisdiction with equivalent data protection law), you have the following rights regarding your personal data. To exercise any of them, write to hello@jordanelias.de. We will respond within 30 days.

Right of access

You have the right to request a copy of the personal data we hold about you and information about how it is used. Most of your data is already visible to you within the platform.

Right to rectification

If any data we hold about you is inaccurate, you have the right to have it corrected. You can update your name and email directly from the Account page. For other data, contact us.

Right to erasure

You have the right to request deletion of your personal data. You can delete individual journal entries and check-ins from within the platform. For full account deletion, see the section below.

Right to data portability

You have the right to receive your data in a structured, machine-readable format. Journal entries can be downloaded as .txt files from within the platform. For a full export of all your data, contact us and we will prepare one.

Right to object / restrict processing

You have the right to object to certain types of processing or to request that processing be restricted while a dispute is resolved. Contact us to discuss.

Lawful basis for processing

We process your data on the following legal bases under GDPR Article 6: performance of a contract (providing the service you subscribed to); legitimate interests (security logging, fraud prevention); and consent (for any processing not covered by the above, you will be asked explicitly). Health-related data (where applicable under Article 9) is processed with your explicit consent.

Deleting your account

You can request full deletion of your account and all associated data at any time by writing to hello@jordanelias.de with the subject line "Account deletion request".

On receiving your request, we will:

Delete your account from Supabase authentication, which removes your email and authentication credentials. Delete all rows in profiles, journal_entries, checkins, shared_items, and any other content tables associated with your user ID. Delete any audio files stored in Supabase Storage under your user ID. Cancel your Stripe subscription if active, and delete your Stripe customer record.

Because your journal content is encrypted with a key tied to your account, deletion of the account also renders any remaining ciphertext permanently undecryptable — even if database backups exist.

We will confirm deletion within 14 days. If you have an active session tier subscription, please also cancel it from the Account page before requesting deletion to avoid further charges.

Important Account deletion is permanent and cannot be undone. All your journal entries, check-in history, and saved content will be permanently deleted. Download anything you want to keep before making this request.
