## 2. Fixes (things that are wrong or inconsistent today)

Ordered by how much they matter before 1 January.

### A. Health and nervous-system claims still on the public pages (high)
You decided on no claims about the nervous system, and the positioning is "Track your progress without being tracked". The home page still says otherwise:
- `index.html`: og and twitter descriptions ("nervous system regulation", "soundscapes for focus, anxiety, studying, and sleep"), structured data (`"applicationCategory": "HealthApplication"`, "anxiety relief", keywords "music for anxiety", "nervous system regulation"), the FAQ answers ("people with anxiety… sleep problems"), module blurbs ("evidence-based breathing exercises for anxiety relief, stress reduction, nervous system regulation") and the about text.
- `breathing/index.html`: "Activates the parasympathetic nervous system…", "used for nervous system regulation", and a paragraph on "autonomic nervous system activity".
- `journal/index.html` prompt: "What does your nervous system need today?"
- `subscribe/index.html`: one line to read.
- Why it matters: efficacy claims are what regulators and the Play Console health questionnaire look at, and a "HealthApplication" tag with treatment-sounding copy invites medical-device-style scrutiny. Fix: reword to what the tools do ("breathing patterns to follow", "sound you can shape") and drop efficacy language; change the structured-data category. **Needs your OK on the wording before I change the home page.**

### B. Inner pages have no meta description and no `<h1>` (medium)
About 30 pages use an `<h2>` as the page title and have no description (the pages behind login are `noindex`, so search matters less, but screen readers, browser history and link previews use them). Fix: promote the page title to `<h1>` (visually identical), add a one-line description to each public page. Mechanical, one branch, tested by the existing link check plus a new "one h1 per page" test.

### C. Sitemap lists two pages (medium)
`sitemap.xml` has only the home page and the privacy page. If other pages are meant to be found (about, therapy, booking), add them; if not, nothing to do. Your call.

### D. The web manifest is not a proper installable-app manifest (medium)
`site.webmanifest` has one 1024 px icon marked `any maskable` (the full favicon, so a maskable crop would cut into it), no `start_url`, `scope`, `lang`, `description` or `id`. The Android app now has correct safe-zone icons; the website manifest should use the same files (192, 512 and a separate maskable one) and a `start_url` of `/dashboard/`. The theme colour differs too (`#2a5c45` on the web, `#1C554E` in the app): pick one.

### E. Experiment progress and notes live only in browser storage (medium)
`sil.exp.done` and `sil.exp.note.*` are in `localStorage`. They disappear if the person clears site data, uses a private window, switches phone, or the Android WebView is reset, and they do not follow the person from web to app. Also: leaving an experiment mid-way restarts it at step 0. Fix: keep a local-first copy, and add an optional sync into the encrypted vault (same privacy model as the journal: we hold only ciphertext). Remember the step position per experiment.

### F. The empty button in the cocktail-party game (low)
`cocktail-party-problem/index.html` has three empty `<button>` choices that are filled by script. They are `disabled` until filled, so harmless, but they have no accessible name before the first round.

### G. Smaller items to verify at the first deploy
- OCR language files (`*.traineddata.gz`): check Netlify does not add `Content-Encoding: gzip`.
- `sw.js` uses a fixed `sil-v1` cache name; it is network-first so it is safe, but bump the name when `PAGES` changes.
- `offline.html` and the cached tool list (`PAGES` in `sw.js`) do not include Compositions, Library or the new Caseload page; decide which should work offline.
- The 10 MB `audio/maskers/traffic.mp3` is the heaviest file on the site. Fine on Wi-Fi; worth a lower-bitrate version for mobile data.

## 3. Making the app more engaging (whole product)

### 3.1 A guided "today" path (highest value)
Today the dashboard opens with one check-in card and a daily prompt, then a flat grid of about 16 tools. There is no sense of "what should I do now", and no first-run introduction. Proposal:
- **First run (once):** three short questions (what brings you here: focus / calm / creativity / track a pattern; how much time you usually have: 5 / 15 / 30 min; do you work with a therapist). Stored locally and used only to order the dashboard. Skippable. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Today card becomes a 5–15 minute path:** check-in, then one tool chosen from the person's answers and recent use, then a one-line note. The existing check-in insight card and the course's "Continue" button are the building blocks.
- **Group the grid** into Calm, Make, Listen/Train, Reflect, so 16 equal tiles become four short lists. Keep a search/all-tools view.
- **Gentle cadence, never a streak that punishes:** "3 days this week" instead of "streak: 0". Optional reminders already exist on Android.

### 3.2 Make sessions feel like sessions
- **Keep the screen awake** during breathing, regulation, timed journal and experiment timers (Screen Wake Lock API; a one-line Capacitor equivalent in the app). Nothing in the codebase uses it today, so the screen may dim mid-exercise.
- **Haptics on Android** for breath phases and step changes (Capacitor Haptics, tiny). Not used anywhere.
- **A calm end of session:** one quiet "done" screen that offers the next small thing (note, check-in, a related tool), instead of returning to a list.
- **Spoken steps (optional, on device):** the browser's speech synthesis can read an experiment step or a breathing cue aloud for eyes-closed practice. No network.

### 3.3 Continuity between tools
- "Recently used" and "pick up where you left off" on the dashboard (local, per device).
- When a tool produces something (a Regulation WAV, a sequencer pattern, an experiment note), offer to keep it in one place (see 4.4) instead of leaving each in its own tool.

## 4. Experiments in Creativity: more immersive, more engaging, more guided

What exists and works well: 36 experiments in 7 units, one step at a time with a progress bar, a tool box per experiment (generators 18, timers 31, rhythm grooves 9, audio clip operations 7, writing tools), "Surprise me", a filter by what you have, a printable book, a notebook export, optional end note, and no scoring. The steps are short (average 63 characters) and all have a stop rule, an on-paper version and an open reflection. That is a strong base. The gaps:

### 4.1 Tools are not tied to the step
All tools for an experiment appear below every step, so on a phone the person scrolls past a metronome to find the dice. Proposal: each step names the tool it needs (`tool: 'gen'`), and only that tool is shown under that step, expanded and ready. Steps with a time ("play for 4 minutes") get an inline timer that starts on tap and gives a soft tone at the end (and a vibration on Android).

### 4.2 A real beginning and a real ending
- **Beginning:** a 20-second "set up" screen: what to have in front of you, a checkbox list, headphones recommended or not, and a quiet "ready" button. Today the first screen is a list of facts.
- **Ending:** the "All done" page asks for a note and offers the next experiment. Add: a short "what you did" recap (the numbers you rolled, the version you chose), the saved output if any (an audio clip, a pattern, a poem), and a clear line to the next experiment in the same unit.
- **Unit completion:** a small marker and one sentence when a unit is finished, and the capstone shown as "unlocked" after Units 1–6 (or at least 4). The capstone is currently a single experiment; a bank of three or four capstones (different limits, different media) would let people choose.

### 4.3 More guidance for starting
- A short "which one for me?" at the top: pick what you have (instrument / recording / DAW / nothing) and time available (10 / 20 / 30 min), get one suggestion. The filter chips exist; this turns them into a recommendation.
- Show the time and need on the "Continue" button ("Continue: The wrong hand · 15 min · nothing but you").
- Return to an interrupted experiment at the step the person left.

### 4.4 A place for what you make
Outputs are currently held in memory and lost when the page closes unless the person downloads them. Add a **Keep** shelf: audio clips, patterns and notes saved on the device (and in the vault when synced), listed by experiment with date, playable and downloadable. This is what makes the course feel cumulative rather than a series of one-offs.

### 4.5 Looking back
On the notebook page, add on-device reflection from the person's own notes: words that recur, which experiments they went back to, what they noticed most after each unit. The note-analysis modules built for the therapist record can be reused. It describes the person's writing; it does not interpret them.

### 4.6 Content gaps (for you to decide)
- Units are uneven: Limits has 4, Melody 8, Capstone 1.
- Only the writing and rhythm units reach "nothing but you". More no-equipment experiments (voice, body percussion, listening walks) would suit phones.
- Experiments that use the other tools (Sequencer, Soundscape, Regulation) via `links` could become guided: "open the Sequencer with this pattern already set".
