Sound moving through space

Each dot in the circle represents a sound source. When you put on headphones, the dot's position maps directly to where you'll hear the sound — left, right, in front, or behind you.

Watch the dot move around the circle and notice how the sound follows it through space.

Introduction · Step 2 of 2

The shell game

You'll see the target dot (filled) and one or two distractor dots (hollow rings) playing at the same time. Then all the dots fade out and keep moving — your job is to track the target through the sound alone.

When the movement stops, the dots reappear — all identical. Tap the one you were tracking.

Headphones are strongly recommended for the full spatial effect.

How spatial hearing works

The auditory system locates sound in space using interaural time differences (ITD) — the tiny delay between a sound reaching each ear — and interaural level differences (ILD), the difference in loudness between ears. For sounds above and below, the shape of the outer ear (the pinna) introduces frequency-dependent filtering that the brain learns to decode as elevation.

Practising active sound tracking exercises the superior colliculus and auditory cortex circuits responsible for reflexive and voluntary spatial attention. The shell game format — watching a target, then selecting its resting position from among identical-looking distractors — separates perceptual tracking from motor response, isolating the attentional component.

This exercise is adapted from research on auditory scene analysis (Bregman, 1990) and the cocktail party effect. Consistent practice improves the ability to selectively attend to a single sound source in noisy environments.
